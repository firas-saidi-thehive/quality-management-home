CTA 01

Explore the Quality Management System

The QMS is the coordinated framework through which The Hive manages the quality of services, processes, projects, teams, and internal operations.

What the QMS brings together

Policies, procedures, roles, controls, performance indicators, risks, internal audits, non-conformities, CAPA, management reviews, lessons learned, and continuous improvement activities.

Why it matters

The objective is not simply documentation. The QMS helps teams identify weaknesses early, protect client commitments, preserve evidence, learn from delivery experience, and improve operational performance.

Our quality principles

Client Focus

Understand requirements, contractual commitments, service expectations, and business priorities.

Process Ownership

Every critical process has an accountable owner for documentation, controls, performance, and improvement.

Evidence-Based Management

Use KPIs, feedback, audits, incidents, risk reviews, and records to guide decisions.

Risk-Based Thinking

Identify possible failure early, strengthen controls, and capture improvement opportunities.

Accountability

Keep roles, approvals, decision rights, and responsibilities clear and traceable.

Controlled Documentation

Use current, approved, accessible documents and archive obsolete versions correctly.

Continuous Improvement

Use experience, trend data, lessons, feedback, and audits to improve how work is done.

How it works

Plan. Do. Check. Improve.

The continuous management cycle connecting QMS governance to everyday service delivery.

01 PLAN

Set direction

Establish quality objectives, roles, process requirements, risks, controls, KPIs, competence, and controlled documents.

02 DO

Deliver

Operate services and projects according to approved procedures, standards, controls, responsibilities, and client commitments.

03 CHECK

Evaluate

Review KPIs, audits, client feedback, incidents, non-conformities, control tests, risks, and documentation quality.

04 IMPROVE

Act & learn

Use CAPA, root-cause analysis, lessons learned, revised controls, training, and optimization initiatives.

CTA 02 • TH-QM-POL-001

Quality Policy

The Hive’s controlled policy establishing the direction, commitments, and expectations for quality management.

Our commitment

The Hive is committed to delivering reliable, secure, consistent, and high-quality services that meet agreed client requirements, support business objectives, and contribute to sustainable long-term performance.

Policy applies to

Internal operations, client service delivery, project delivery, enterprise support and AMS, technology services, management activities, support functions, employees, managers, contractors, and external resources where relevant.

Policy objectives

  • Deliver consistent and reliable services.
  • Meet client and contractual requirements.
  • Maintain controlled and measurable processes.
  • Manage risks and improvement opportunities.
  • Correct weaknesses and prevent recurrence.
  • Develop employee competence.
  • Maintain document control and reliable evidence.
  • Improve the organizational system continuously.

Performance

Quality objectives and indicators measure client satisfaction, SLA performance, process effectiveness, audit compliance, CAPA closure, and risk reduction.

Prevention

Significant, repeated, or systemic issues receive formal investigation and corrective action to reduce recurrence.

Policy review

The policy is reviewed annually or after a significant change affecting strategy, operations, client requirements, risks, or technology.

CTA 03

Submit a Quality Issue

A quality issue is a broad concern, weakness, deviation, recurring problem, control gap, client concern, or improvement opportunity.

When to raise an issue

  • A process did not happen as expected.
  • An approved procedure, standard, or control was not followed.
  • Documentation is missing, unclear, incorrect, or outdated.
  • A client may be affected by delay, inconsistency, or error.
  • A KPI repeatedly misses its target.
  • A recurring weakness, supplier issue, or better way of working is identified.

What happens after reporting

Report → Quality Management reviews the available facts → the item is classified → an accountable owner is assigned → actions are investigated and verified → the record is closed and learned from.

Possible classifications: Observation, Improvement Opportunity, Quality Issue, Non-Conformity, Major Non-Conformity, CAPA, or Risk.

Important: A Quality Issue does not automatically become a formal Non-Conformity or CAPA. Quality Management determines the appropriate route based on evidence, impact, recurrence, urgency, and risk.

CTA 04

Quality Management FAQs

Common questions about the QMS, documentation, ownership, reporting, audit, CAPA, KPI, and risk management.

What is the Quality Management System?

The QMS is the framework used to manage and improve services, processes, controls, and internal operations. It connects policies, procedures, responsibilities, objectives, KPIs, risks, audits, non-conformities, CAPA, management reviews, lessons learned, and continuous improvement.

Is The Hive ISO 9001 certified?

The QMS uses structured ISO-style quality principles. Unless an official company announcement confirms certification, employees must not represent The Hive as ISO 9001 certified.

What is the difference between a Quality Issue and a Non-Conformity?

A Quality Issue is a broad reporting mechanism for a concern, weakness, or opportunity. A Non-Conformity is a confirmed or evidence-supported failure to meet an identified requirement such as a policy, procedure, control, SLA, contract, standard, or regulation.

What does CAPA mean?

CAPA means Corrective and Preventive Action. It is a formal process used for significant, recurring, systemic, high-risk, client-impacting, or compliance-related issues where root-cause analysis and effectiveness verification are required.

Where do I find approved policies and procedures?

Use the official Process & Procedure Library. The current approved SharePoint version is authoritative; locally saved, emailed, or out-of-date files must not be treated as the official reference.

Is an Internal Audit a personal performance evaluation?

No. An Internal Audit evaluates processes, controls, documentation, evidence, and management-system effectiveness. It is not intended as an employee performance review.

CTA 06

Report a Non-Conformity

A Non-Conformity occurs when objective evidence shows that a defined requirement was not met.

What can be a Non-Conformity?

Failure against a policy, procedure, standard, process, control, contract, SLA, client commitment, audit criterion, supplier requirement, or applicable legal obligation.

Minor Non-Conformity

A limited, isolated failure such as a one-off documentation error, single missed approval, or low-impact process deviation.

Major Non-Conformity

A significant, systemic, repeated, high-risk, client-impacting, contractual, compliance, or control failure. It normally requires escalation and may require CAPA.

Non-Conformity lifecycle

IdentifyRegisterAssessContainInvestigateRoot CauseCorrective ActionImplementVerifyEffectiveness ReviewClose

The record should capture the affected requirement, evidence, impact, severity, recurrence, immediate containment, root cause, action owner, due date, CAPA link, risk link, verification result, and closure decision.

CTA 07

CAPA Register & Management

Corrective and Preventive Action is used when an issue needs a structured, evidence-based response beyond a simple correction.

When CAPA is considered

  • Major, recurring, systemic, high-risk, or client-impacting Non-Conformity.
  • Significant audit finding, control failure, incident trend, or KPI deterioration.
  • Failed previous corrective action, supplier failure, management review decision, or serious risk.

CAPA lifecycle

Initiate → Assess → Contain → Investigate → Root-Cause Analysis → Define Action Plan → Approve → Implement → Verify → Effectiveness Review → Close.

Root-cause approaches may include 5 Whys, cause-and-effect analysis, timeline analysis, process analysis, interviews, evidence review, and data analysis.

My CAPAs

CAPAs owned by the current responsible person.

High & Critical

CAPAs requiring increased management attention.

Awaiting Verification

Actions complete; evidence requires quality verification.

Effectiveness Review

Actions are being monitored to confirm recurrence was prevented.

CTA 10

Internal Audit Center

Internal audits provide evidence-based assurance that processes, policies, controls, documentation, and operational practices operate as intended.

Audit principles

  • Evidence-based: conclusions rely on verifiable records and observations.
  • Objective: audit evidence is assessed without predetermined conclusions.
  • Risk-based: audit priority reflects process criticality, client impact, and previous performance.
  • Independent where practical: auditors avoid reviewing activity they directly own.
  • Improvement-oriented: audits identify strengths and opportunities as well as failures.

Audit lifecycle

Plan → Prepare → Conduct → Evaluate Evidence → Discuss Findings → Report → Correct → Follow Up → Verify Effectiveness → Close.

Findings can be classified as Conformity, Observation, Opportunity for Improvement, Minor Non-Conformity, Major Non-Conformity, or Good Practice.

Audit output: Audit reports should capture scope, criteria, evidence, findings, classification, owners, due dates, required actions, related NC/CAPA records, and follow-up verification.

CTA 11

Risks & Opportunities

Risk-based thinking helps The Hive identify uncertainty early, maintain controls, protect client commitments, and take advantage of meaningful improvement opportunities.

Risk lifecycle

Identify → Assess → Prioritize → Treat → Monitor → Review → Close.

A useful risk statement describes: Cause → Risk Event → Potential Impact. Example: a dependency on one escalation owner could delay client response during absence and result in SLA breaches.

Risk assessment and treatment

Likelihood and impact use a 1–5 scale. The risk score is Likelihood × Impact. Treatments may be Avoid, Reduce, Transfer, or Accept, with an accountable owner, target date, evidence, and review schedule.

Opportunities may relate to automation, client experience, control improvement, cost optimization, performance improvement, or knowledge management.

Open Risks

Active quality-related risks requiring monitoring.

High & Critical

Risks requiring management attention and treatment.

Risk Actions

Active risk treatment actions and overdue commitments.

Open Opportunities

Improvement opportunities under assessment or implementation.
